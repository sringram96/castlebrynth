# CASTLEBRYNTH — the game, from above

*(The pitch, verbatim. Where `.claude/rules/` conflicts with this
document — valued BRACE for "eats one blow," talismans for "jokers,"
the pouch-and-hand for the fixed five-plus-signature — the rules win.
The pitch stays for the why.)*

**One line:** a dark descent where curiosity is free, choices are
expensive, and death teaches you the map — a Layton adventure
falling into a dice-fight roguelike, played with one thumb, in the
dark.

## the fantasy

You wake at a cold crossing beneath Castlebrynth with a pouch of
bones and a name you keep saying: your brother's. The labyrinth
goes down. It does not go back up. Doors close behind you like
verdicts, candles do the only arguing against the dark, and
everything you learn — every lie the walls told, every thing you
refused to take — you keep, even when the labyrinth kills you.
Especially then.

## two engines, one doorway

The game is two machines joined at a single hinge:

**THE DESCENT** — exploration. Still rooms, painted like plates,
read like a novel a candle at a time. Tap anything: tapping is
always safe and always answers — curiosity is never punished.
Acting is different: take, use, speak, PEER into the dark, LISTEN
at a door — actions have gates and consequences, and some of what
you refuse is remembered in a Refused Ledger the labyrinth keeps.
Each room ends at 2–3 doors, each sensed in one line ("behind
this one, something wet is scratching"). Doors are the map. You
choose forward. There is no back.

**THE LOTS** — combat, and the only way through certain doors is
to fight. Your hand is five bones plus a signature die you chose
at your first waking. Dice are loaded and honest about it — every
lean declared when you inspect them. Enemies never roll: their
INTENT is shown before you act, every turn. You cast, then make
one decision: THROW a combo (sum × tier in harm), BRACE (hold a
die — a held die eats one blow), or FLEE (always offered; some
things pursue). Jokers add, spells interrupt, skills bend a roll
after it lands. Your stats are your body; the dice are your hands.

The doorway between the engines is the fight itself: exploration
delivers you to violence, violence returns you to exploration,
and the same forward-only ratchet governs both.

## death is the spine

Two ledgers. The RUN ledger — items, health, sanity, position —
burns when you die, and either bar at zero is death, same rule.
The PERMANENT ledger survives: your dice, your signature, marked
keepsakes, everything you know, everything you refused. **Dice
survive death.** Each death writes one line in the Book of Ends,
reseeds the doors, and wakes you at the Crossing — poorer in
things, richer in the only currency that compounds: knowledge of
what is down there. The player's real progression is the map in
their head and the calluses on their hand.

## the truth (SPOILERS — depth 3 only)

Your brother was not taken. He was *prepared* — a vessel for the
Morning King, and the labyrinth is the throat he must be carried
down. The infant-shaped horrors in the deep places are not
accidents of design; they rhyme with what waits at the bottom,
and a player who has been paying attention will feel wrong about
fighting them long before they can say why. The game never states
this before tier three. The dread arrives before the sentence.

## how it looks and sounds

Hand-authored pixel art at hero-plate quality: rooms on GRID-240,
cinema moments on CINE-480, drawn under a strict doctrine —
planes not lighting, authored silhouettes, heavy outlines, detail
hoarded at eyes and mouths and hands, everything else quiet and
dark (a third of every frame is black). Scenes are STILLS; life
comes from small region patches on desynced clocks — a flame, a
drool strand, an eye that finds you — and the rare motions that
matter never undo themselves: the creature that lurches two
pixels closer stays two pixels closer. The prose is the sound
design for v1: canon voice, second person, present tense, no "you
feel," mercy nowhere. One candle of text at a time.

## platform and shape

A phone game, portrait, letterboxed, played with one thumb.
Browser-first (a URL is the whole install), wrapped native later.
Sessions are short pushes: wake, go down three rooms, die
interestingly, put the phone away changed. Close it mid-anything;
it resumes exactly there.

## scope

**v1 — the first descent:** the Crossing, depth one, keys and
gates, a handful of horrors, the Warden at the bottom, death done
right, the two ledgers true. Small, finished, frightening.

**the long game (backlog prose, not promises):** depths two and
three and the truth; the Tenant in re-visited rooms; the
knucklebones spend economy; QTE parries; grants and travelers and
merchants; the semantic layer; the Fraying. Every one a plug into
a named socket. None of it before the skeleton walks.

## why it works (the design thesis)

Three honesty mechanics carry the whole game. The tap/act split
makes curiosity free and commitment costly, so reading the world
is joy and touching it is drama. Declared leans and shown intents
make every fight a fair puzzle, so dread comes from the situation,
never from hidden math. And the forward-only ratchet plus
knowledge-that-survives makes death itself the progression system
— the labyrinth wins every run, and teaches you how to beat it by
doing so. The player descends; the game remembers; both are
changed. That's the adventure.
