# The thumb — the interaction model
Articles keep their numbers; cite as "art. N". Statuses as in
the-world.md. This file governs how the game is touched; it stands
beside the touch laws (arts 5–8, the-world.md) and the screen laws
(arts 29–30, the-room.md). Ratified 2026-08-04 from the first
skeleton playtest.

## The two registers
66. SETTLED — Controls and prose are different languages. A control is
    a plain imperative verb, two words or fewer: Take. Open. Fight.
    Run. Roll. End turn. Prose never instructs; controls never
    narrate. The poetry is the response to the button, never the
    button. Controls are exempt from the prose register in
    rules/voice.md and bound by this article instead.

## The tray
67. SETTLED — The tray is anatomy, not a menu. Fixed regions, always
    in the same places: vitals (health, armor); the pouch as visible
    slots, empty slots included — hand size is a stat you can see;
    the act strip; the card's glyph (art. 74). Nothing else ever
    appears in the tray.

## Look, then take
68. SETTLED (ruled) — A tap investigates; an act commits. Tapping
    anything — a possession, a thing in the world, a door, the
    horror's intent — is free, always answers, and never commits.
    Every commitment (take, equip, open, drink, fight, run) is a
    deliberate plain-verb act in the act strip. "Inspect" buttons do
    not exist; inspection is what tapping is. A possession's answer
    is its declared truth (art. 54: odds, riders, effects, costs).
69. SETTLED — Silence is a bug. Every tappable thing answers with at
    least a line. No dead pixels among the tappable.
70. SETTLED — The world remembers in pixels. Any act that changes
    state changes the scene: the taken key is gone from the floor,
    the opened door stands open, the wounded horror stays wounded.
    Prose confirms; pixels prove. (Extends art. 28.)
71. SETTLED — A bare tap never walks you through a door. Forward
    being forever (art. 9), every irreversible act goes through the
    act strip as a plain verb you pressed.

## The dice under the thumb
72. SETTLED (ruled) — Selection is staged, and kept-marks are mortal.
    During the keep phase, kept dice are unmistakably marked. When
    the second casting lands, all keep-marks clear — the hand after
    the recast is just the hand, and which dice survived the first
    cast is no longer information. In the claim phase, idle,
    selected, and claimed are unmistakably distinct states; unused
    dice dim at resolve. DEFAULT within this article: claim offers
    match the exact selection, and a selection that fits nothing says
    why ("no combo uses exactly these dice").
73. SETTLED — The intent is tappable. Its declared effect explains
    itself in plain words on touch. Fairness you can interrogate
    (extends arts 42, 65).

## Persistence & the card
74. SETTLED (ruled) — The card lives behind the glyph. A small,
    persistent info glyph — the way Balatro keeps hand stats aside —
    opens the card in one tap. The card is never parked mid-screen;
    placement of the glyph is the design lead's within this
    constraint.
75. SETTLED — Interaction is state. A half-made selection, a
    half-read scene, a half-spent turn all survive the lock screen
    (art. 36 applied to the fingertip).

## The interaction budget
76. SETTLED (ruled) — No interaction type is banned, and none enters
    cheaply. The tap is the entire necessary vocabulary. A new
    interaction type — long-press, drag, gesture, tilt — enters the
    game only by winning a stand-up with a stated, compelling reason,
    and its admission is recorded as an article in this file. The
    parallax lean (art. 8) remains parked and remains eligible.
