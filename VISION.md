## the two halves — two engines, one doorway

HALF 1 — THE LOTS (resolution): dice combat and everything it spends.
HALF 2 — THE DESCENT (navigation): Layton investigation on StS
structure. Merchant and dialogue encounters resolve inside the
Descent; FIGHT is the only doorway into the Lots. The Descent finds
what the Lots spend.

## the descent (navigation — rulings from this review)

- **FORWARD-ONLY** (player-ratified): no backtracking within a push.
  The knowledge ratchet is CROSS-PUSH: refusals and clues logged this
  push pay off when their kind is met again on later pushes. Walker
  law: within a push, any clue-locked gate must draw its clue earlier
  OR be cross-push knowledge by design.
- **Doors are the map** (fleet consensus for N1): always see the next
  2-3 doors as sensed descriptions (light, sound, smell) — one step
  of lookahead, in-frame, no map screen ever.
- **Draw cadence** (N3 default): rooms drawn fresh each push from the
  authored pool; landmarks fixed per depth.
- Rooms: free-tap investigation — clues, information, secrets that
  refuse you (ledger), telegraphed instant deaths, encounters
  (merchant / dialogue / fight). Every event carries dialogue; QTEs
  are their own category.

## the lots (combat — dice model v2, this review)

- **Your hand: 5 regular bones + 1 SIGNATURE DIE — chosen, not
  given.** At the Crossing several bones lie where you woke; the
  player selects one ("one of these was yours" — the game never
  confirms). The selection IS the class system. First speced class:
  THE WIZARD — the Hollow Die, five blank faces + one face of power;
  blanks roll dead, and the wizard sockets collected spells onto
  blank faces over the campaign (weakest start, hardest scale).
  Launch roster of other signature dice: OPEN.
- **Loaded bones (KCD-style):** every die keeps all six numbers;
  weighting is a declared probability LEAN ("favors 3 — lands about
  twice as often"), stated on inspect and legible in the art
  (drilled pips, lead-dark faces, a bone that sits wrong). No face
  is ever eliminated. Plus at most ONE gilded trigger face per die
  ("gilded 3: +3 when scored") — bias and gilding may stack on rare
  finds. Verb-faces with self-costs remain SCRAPPED.
- **Collecting dice is rare and ceremonial:** a new die is never
  floor loot. EXACTLY ONE notable-die event per depth — guarded,
  priced, or bargained; win, pay, or walk away.
- **The collection is permanent — RATIFIED LAW.** Dice survive
  death, live in the stash; the pouch (5 + signature) is packed at
  the Crossing; mid-push finds are swap-or-leave. TWO LEDGERS: the
  run ledger (tithes, consumables, non-kept gear — death takes it)
  and the campaign ledger (dice, knowledge, landmarks, grants —
  death cannot touch it). The filling ossuary is the campaign's
  power curve — the role XP used to want.
- Combos multiply (pair through five-of-a-kind, chunky tiers);
  base = sum of thrown faces; items are additive jokers; held dice
  block 1 each. **Enemies do not roll** — deterministic telegraphed
  intents (elites may carry two-value intents).
- **Spells are instants (MTG-style). Mana is dead.** Non-wizards:
  spells are CONSUMABLES (scrolls/vials, one cast, the two
  small-things slots, sold by merchants). The wizard sockets spells
  onto the Hollow Die instead — rolling the face is the cost.
- **Currency stays load-bearing:** tithes buy consumables (incl.
  spell-instants), armor, and upgrades at merchants — the run
  ledger's engine.
- OPEN: spend economy (knucklebones gather vs refresh-per-fight —
  decide on the fight-screen mock, by thumb); launch signature-die
  roster beyond the wizard; semantic layer (retry later, from play
  outward).

RELIABILITY (combo-rate via composition), not raw EV — bands hold
while felt power soars; specialists keep natural blind spots (no
1-2s = no low straights), preserving F6.
